Finding Quick Wins. Let's discuss how to get quick wins with machine learning. What are the low-hanging fruits in an organisation?

One quick win is improving help desks. Many simple requests don’t need a human response, and the user will be happy to get their issue fixed quickly rather than waiting. A chatbot can sit as the first point of contact and take easily quickly answered questions like “How do I reset my password?” This response is a natural language processing solution.

A recommender system is another quick win. If your company has a central document control system, you will likely find that employees in certain job roles search for similar documents. Speeding up this process with a recommender system can save a lot of time across the company.

Not all company information will be nicely structured and neatly stored. Vast quantities of unstructured data will nevertheless contain important information. These might be emails, presentations, or text documents.

Creating a query: “How do I render video files?” won’t just directly match the terms included in the search, such as video or render, but will also look for deeper connections, such as video rendering requiring a greater need for RAM or graphics card. These are connections learned by the system from past user searches on this subject.

As a result, creating a semantic search in this way will speed up finding information and prevent unintentional duplication of documents because they cannot be found. A semantic search can find specific details from a human-readable question.

All organisations will have reports based on traditional data analytics, giving insight into business performance. These may point to datasets where there is a clear target to predict where ML might find patterns that have yet to be found up till now.

No two ML projects are the same so much data practitioner work is research and development. Try things out and run trials. An organisation with a robust data culture will not only achieve quick wins but also support Research & Development, fostering innovation and growth.
